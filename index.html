The puzzle

The old measure of ability

  • Political efficacy is usually measured on a scale: do you feel able to influence government, yes or no.
  • For a young woman weighing what her family expects of her, that "ability" may not start at the ballot box.

It may start at the dinner table — in what she is allowed to say out loud, and to whom.

Muslim-majority efficacy study, 2025

My research question

How do young women in Kazakhstan describe the role of family expectations in shaping their sense of ability to participate in or influence politics?

Fall 2026

Where I stand epistemologically

Interpretivism

Research that asks what "ability" means to a woman inside her own family — not how to score it on a scale.

  • It fits because efficacy has already been measured on a scale elsewhere, quantitatively, from the outside.
  • A survey would decide in advance what "influence" looks like, before she gets the chance to say.

Schwartz-Shea & Yanow, 2012

What this adds

A small, situated picture

  • One group: young women in Kazakhstan, Fall 2026.
  • The link between patriarchal norms and lower political efficacy is already established — across Muslim-majority societies broadly, and in post-Soviet Russia specifically.
  • I aim that "own words" approach at Kazakhstan, and at family expectations, specifically.

My lens · four loose starting ideas

Sensitising concepts

Family expectation
what a "good daughter" is or isn't allowed to do
Internal political efficacy
the felt capacity to understand or influence politics
Voice vs. silence
speaking up, or staying quiet, as a choice
Patriarchal bargain
negotiating inside constraints, not just obeying or refusing them

What I expect (and could be wrong)

My expectations

  1. More about implicit permission from family than formal political knowledge.
  2. "Ability to influence" described in private, household terms before public ones.
  3. A tension between loyalty to family and her own political voice.

Interpretivist does not mean having no expectations.
